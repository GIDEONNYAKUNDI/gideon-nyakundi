About Dr. Gideon

A son of Manga who learned to fix systems

Gideon Moseti Nyakundi's umbilical cord was cut at Manga. He grew up in Nyamira's villages and was born into the Abagusii. As a boy he was being prepared to become an omobari, a traditional healer of the head. From age four to nine he watched that practice closely and learned resilience from it. He was also the child called on to tear Ekerundu to stop the rain at ceremonies.

When modern life ended that tradition, Gideon turned to formal schooling and became a clinical engineer. He kept the lessons of whole-body healing he grew up with and joined them to modern medicine.

He then worked his way up through some of the most demanding health systems in the United States. He started at the bench repairing life-saving equipment, moved into operating theatres and hospital operations, and became a senior health executive. He has worked on the evaluation of the Affordable Care Act and has studied health systems across the world.

He has written five books, on Kenyan healthcare, global health policy, the Kenyan school system and Gusii history and song. Each book asks the same question he now brings to Nyamira: what would it take to make this system work for ordinary families?

Nyamira Today

Fertile highlands, unfinished promises

Nyamira is one of Kenya's greenest counties, with tea and bananas on red volcanic soil and rivers running to Lake Victoria. Many families still lack safe water, reliable medicine and a road that stays open in the rains.

The Challenges

Where the money goes, and where it does not

The county's 2026/27 budget plans for Ksh 8.85 billion. About 76% of that comes from the national equitable share. Seven of every ten shillings go to recurrent costs, and the services people use most have been cut hardest.

Planned vs. funded, 2026/27 development

Water: Ksh 55M funded of Ksh 441M planned12%
Medical services: Ksh 340M of Ksh 1,555M requested22%
Crop development: Ksh 145M of Ksh 375M planned39%
Roads & public works: Ksh 489M of Ksh 798M planned61%
Health products fund: Ksh 100M of Ksh 500M proposed20%

Source: Nyamira County Fiscal Strategy Paper 2026/27, popular version [4]

What residents tell us

"There are no drugs." Patients arrive at Level 4 hospitals and are sent to buy medicine and supplies in private chemists.

Roads that swallow vehicles. In November 2025 residents marched on the Manga–Mote–Momwamu road, where vehicles stick in mud every rainy season despite years of promises to tarmac it [5].

Unpaid debts that kill local business. More than Ksh 260 million of the county's pending bills are over two years old. Local contractors and suppliers who did the work are still waiting [3].

Own revenue is collapsing. Locally collected revenue was budgeted at Ksh 300M in 2024/25 and Ksh 150M in 2025/26. That leaves the county almost entirely dependent on Nairobi [4].

Young people with no work. Graduates and school-leavers ride boda bodas or leave for Nairobi and Kisumu because there are no jobs at home.

01
Pillar One

Healthcare360 Nyamira

A county health system that works from the village health promoter to the referral hospital.

The challenge

  • Medicine stock-outs send patients to private chemists. The county's health products fund for 2026/27 was cut to Ksh 100M from Ksh 500M proposed [4].
  • The medical services development budget is Ksh 340M against Ksh 1.55 billion requested, which delays CT and MRI scanners, X-ray, eye units and oxygen plants [4].
  • There are only 14 health workers per 10,000 residents, and maternal mortality is 349 per 100,000 births [2].
  • Hospital equipment breaks down and stays broken, with no maintenance plan.
Ksh 100M

Health products fund ceiling 2026/27, down from Ksh 500M proposed

The solution

  1. Drugs on every shelf. Restore and ring-fence the health products fund, buy through KEMSA and pooled county procurement, and track stock in every facility on a public dashboard.
  2. A county biomedical engineering unit. This is Gideon's own field: an inventory of every machine, planned maintenance, and repair within 72 hours, so equipment we have paid for works.
  3. Make Level 4 hospitals real Level 4s. Phase in theatres, oxygen, imaging and specialist clinics at Manga, Ekerenyo, Esani and the other sub-county hospitals.
  4. Pay and keep health workers. Clear salary arrears, pay on time, offer rural incentives and train community health promoters in every village.
  5. Make SHA work for families. Run a help desk in every hospital to register patients, file claims correctly and stop SHA money leaking away.
  6. Partner with the diaspora. Bring visiting specialist medical missions and telemedicine links with partner hospitals abroad.

Pledge: no patient in a county facility will be told to buy basic essential medicines elsewhere.

02
Pillar Two

Roads That Last

Every farm, school and clinic within reach of a road that stays open in the long rains.

The challenge

  • Feeder roads turn to mud every rainy season, and tea and bananas rot before they reach buyers.
  • Residents protested on the Manga–Mote–Momwamu road in November 2025 after years of unfulfilled tarmac promises [5].
  • The 2026/27 roads development ceiling is Ksh 489M against Ksh 798M planned [4].
  • Roads are graded once and then left to wash away. Culverts and bridges fail.
61%

Share of the planned roads development budget actually funded, 2026/27

The solution

  1. A county road map published by ward. Every road is graded on condition and on economic value (tea centres, markets, schools, health facilities), and the priority list is made public.
  2. Build once, maintain always. Murram with proper drainage, culverts and stone pitching, plus a ward-based routine maintenance crew paid from the Roads Maintenance Levy.
  3. Our own machinery. A county plant pool of graders, rollers and tippers, shared fairly across the four constituencies.
  4. Hold national government to its promises. Push KeRRA and KeNHA for the trunk roads, including Manga–Mote–Momwamu, with joint county–national tracking.
  5. Local contractors, paid on time. Reserve labour-based works for youth and women groups, and pay certified work within 60 days.
  6. Bridges and footbridges on river crossings where children cross to school.

Pledge: every road contract, its cost and its progress posted online and on the ward notice board.

03
Pillar Three

Clean Water for Every Home

Our hills are full of springs and rivers. No family should walk for dirty water.

The challenge

  • Only 51.5% of households have safe drinking water [2].
  • The 2026/27 water development ceiling is Ksh 55M against Ksh 441M planned, the deepest cut of any major sector [4].
  • Water projects stall half-built, and boreholes break and are abandoned.
  • Women and girls lose hours each day fetching water, and waterborne disease fills clinics.
Ksh 55M

Water development ceiling, 2026/27, vs Ksh 441M in the Annual Development Plan

The solution

  1. Protect every spring. A spring-protection programme in all 20 wards, built with local labour and owned by community water committees.
  2. Finish what was started. Audit every stalled water project in the first 100 days and complete the viable ones before new ones begin.
  3. Expand piped water. Strengthen NYAWASCO, extend gravity schemes from the highlands, and meter supply fairly.
  4. Water at every school and health facility, with rainwater harvesting tanks and handwashing stations.
  5. Sanitation in market centres. Public toilets, solid waste collection and planned sewer lines for Nyamira and Keroka towns.

Pledge: raise safe water access step by step every year and publish the figure annually.

04
Pillar Four

Farming That Pays

Most Nyamira families farm. Farming should make them richer, not poorer.

The challenge

  • Tea and bananas are our main cash crops [1], but farmers sell raw produce at low farm-gate prices to middlemen.
  • The crop development budget for 2026/27 is Ksh 145M, down from Ksh 318M the year before [4].
  • Farms keep shrinking through subdivision, soil is tired, and fertiliser and seed are expensive.
  • There are too few extension officers, and farmers face pests and climate shocks alone.
73%

Share of households engaged in farming [2]

The solution

  1. Value addition at home. Banana processing (flour, crisps, ripening chambers) and cooling and aggregation centres, so more of each bunch's value stays in Nyamira.
  2. Fair tea. Back smallholder tea growers on governance at their factories, timely bonus payments and access to direct markets.
  3. High-value crops for small plots. Hass avocado, coffee revival, horticulture and indigenous vegetables, with certified seedlings.
  4. Dairy that earns. Artificial insemination services, fodder banks, milk coolers and cooperative processing.
  5. An extension officer in every ward, plus plant clinics and soil testing.
  6. Targeted subsidies for fertiliser and inputs, delivered on time for planting and registered so they reach real farmers.

Pledge: one functioning agro-processing or aggregation centre in each constituency within the first term.

05
Pillar Five

Youth Jobs and Enterprise

Our young people are educated and ready. They need work, tools and a fair chance.

The challenge

  • Graduates and school-leavers find few formal jobs in the county.
  • Many young men turn to boda boda work without training, insurance or savings, and many young people migrate to the cities.
  • Youth funds are small, politically allocated and rarely repaid.
  • Drug and alcohol abuse follows idleness.

The solution

  1. Nyamira Digital Work Hubs. Internet, power and training for online and remote work in each constituency, linked to diaspora employers.
  2. Apprenticeships with county contracts. Every county works contract carries a quota of trained local youth.
  3. A transparent Youth Enterprise Fund with business training, mentoring and published lists of beneficiaries.
  4. Boda boda professionalisation. Licensing support, rider training, group insurance and savings SACCOs.
  5. Health and care careers. Train young people as community health promoters, biomedical technicians and care workers, at home and for global markets.
  6. Rehabilitation and prevention programmes for drug and alcohol abuse, run with churches and community groups.

Pledge: a public annual report of every young person trained, placed or funded, by ward.

06
Pillar Six

Education and Skills

From the first ECDE classroom to the vocational college, every child learns and every youth gains a trade.

The challenge

  • Secondary-school attendance was 47.9% in 2015/16 [2].
  • ECDE centres lack trained teachers, meals and materials, and ECDE teachers' terms are insecure.
  • Vocational training centres are underfunded and teach outdated trades.
  • Bursaries are thinly spread and often allocated by patronage.
47.9%

Secondary net attendance, 2015/16 [2]

The solution

  1. Model ECDE in every ward. Trained teachers on secure terms, a school meal and learning materials, following the early-years framework in Gideon's book on the Kenyan school system.
  2. Modern vocational colleges. Re-equip vocational training centres for solar, electrical work, ICT, agro-processing, construction and healthcare technology.
  3. A fair, digital bursary system. Needs-based scoring and published lists, with priority for orphans, children with disabilities and the poorest homes.
  4. Back to school. Community follow-up for children who drop out, and mentorship for girls and boys.
  5. University and diaspora scholarships funded by partners and alumni.

Pledge: every bursary beneficiary list published by ward, every year.

07
Pillar Seven

Clean Money, Open Books

Every shilling belongs to the people of Nyamira. They have a right to see where it goes.

The challenge

  • Pending bills stood at Ksh 582.7M on 30 June 2025, and Ksh 261M of that was more than two years old [3].
  • About 76% of the 2026/27 revenue comes from the national equitable share. Own-source revenue fell from Ksh 300M budgeted in 2024/25 to Ksh 150M in 2025/26 [4].
  • Recurrent costs take about 70% of spending, leaving about 30% for development [4].
  • Residents see money allocated but few finished projects.
Ksh 582.7M

Total pending bills, 30 June 2025 [3]

The solution

  1. Pay what we owe. A verified, published schedule to clear genuine pending bills, starting with the oldest and the smallest local suppliers.
  2. Open budget, open contracts. Every tender, contract and payment posted online in plain language.
  3. Digital revenue collection. Cashless payment for cess, market fees, permits and rates, so revenue reaches the county account and not someone's pocket.
  4. A lean, professional public service. A staff audit, recruitment on merit and performance contracts for every department.
  5. Ward development forums where citizens choose and monitor projects, as the constitution intends.
  6. Zero tolerance for corruption. Prompt referrals to EACC, and the Governor's own wealth declaration made public.

Pledge: a public Governor's scorecard every quarter showing money in, money out and projects done.

08
Pillar Eight

Women, Families and Care

A strong county is built on safe mothers, thriving women and cared-for elders.

The challenge

  • Maternal mortality is 349 per 100,000 live births [2], even though most mothers deliver in health facilities.
  • Women do most of the farm work but have little access to credit, land titles or markets.
  • Elders, widows and people with disabilities are often left without support.
  • Child poverty affects about one in three children [2].
349

Maternal deaths per 100,000 live births, 2019 [2]

The solution

  1. Safe motherhood. 24-hour maternity units, blood availability, emergency obstetric transport and follow-up by community health promoters.
  2. Women's enterprise. Table-banking support, market stalls and procurement opportunities reserved for women's groups.
  3. Care for elders and people with disabilities. A county register, home visits and assistive devices.
  4. Child protection and nutrition in ECDE and at health facilities.
  5. Gender-based violence response desks at hospitals with trained staff and safe referral.

Pledge: women and youth groups receive their legally reserved share of county procurement, and the results are published.

09
Pillar Nine

Safe and Ready County

When disaster strikes, Nyamira will have a plan, trained people and vehicles that start.

The challenge

  • Heavy rains bring landslides, flooding and road accidents, and the response is slow and improvised.
  • Ambulances are few, poorly coordinated and often off the road.
  • Market fires spread because fire-fighting capacity is limited.
  • There is no county-wide emergency number or dispatch system.

The solution

This is Gideon's daily work. As an Emergency Management Coordinator he writes emergency operations plans, analyses hazards, and runs multi-agency mass-casualty exercises with police, ambulance services, schools and hospitals.

  1. A Nyamira Emergency Operations Plan built from a county hazard vulnerability analysis covering landslides, floods, fire, disease outbreaks and road crashes.
  2. One emergency number, one dispatch centre coordinating every county and partner ambulance.
  3. An ambulance in every ward catchment, maintained and staffed, with trained emergency medical technicians.
  4. Fire and rescue units for Nyamira, Keroka, Ekerenyo, Nyansiongo and the major markets.
  5. Drills and training for schools, markets and health facilities, with community first-aid volunteers.

Pledge: a tested county emergency plan within the first year in office.

10
Pillar Ten

Land, Climate and Energy

Protect the hills, rivers and soil that feed us, and bring power to every home.

The challenge

  • Only 43.2% of households have electricity [2].
  • Steep slopes, deforestation and encroachment on river banks cause soil loss and landslides.
  • Changing rains hurt tea, bananas and maize yields.
  • Land disputes and unclear titles hold back investment.
43.2%

Households with electricity, 2019 [2]

The solution

  1. Power for homes and markets. Work with Kenya Power and REREC on last-mile connections, and add solar mini-grids and solar street lighting in market centres.
  2. A county tree and riparian programme with schools and youth groups planting indigenous trees and bamboo on hills and river banks.
  3. Climate-smart farming: terracing, water harvesting and resilient crop varieties, using county climate funds.
  4. Land clinics to help families with succession, surveying and titles.
  5. Clean cooking through improved stoves and biogas for dairy farmers.

Pledge: climate funds spent in the open, ward by ward, with community oversight.

11
Pillar Eleven

Markets, Towns and Investment

Planned towns and modern markets where business grows and traders are respected.

The challenge

  • Traders sell in open, muddy markets without shade, water, toilets or lighting.
  • Towns are growing without plans, drainage or waste collection.
  • Investors find few incentives and slow licensing.
  • The two municipalities collect little revenue: about Ksh 96M combined in 2026/27 [4].

The solution

  1. Modern market centres with sheds, water, toilets, lighting and cold storage, starting with the busiest markets in each constituency.
  2. Planned towns. Spatial plans, drainage, street lighting and waste management for Nyamira and Keroka municipalities and the growing centres.
  3. A one-stop business licensing centre with online permits.
  4. A Nyamira Investment Forum to market tea, banana, dairy, avocado and tourism opportunities to Kenyan and diaspora investors.
  5. Lake Region Economic Bloc partnerships for regional markets and shared infrastructure.

Pledge: traders are consulted before any market is redesigned or relocated.

12
Pillar Twelve

Gusii Culture, Sport and Diaspora

Proud of where we come from, connected to where our people have gone.

The challenge

  • Gusii songs, stories and traditions are fading among the young.
  • Talented athletes and artists have no facilities or pathways.
  • Nyamira's diaspora, in Kenya's cities and abroad, sends money home but has no formal way to invest or help.

The solution

  1. A Gusii Heritage Centre to record and teach Ekegusii songs, dance, oral history and traditional knowledge. Gideon has documented much of this in Where the Hills Remember and The Gusii People: Culture and Songs.
  2. Cultural festivals and creative economy support for musicians, artists and craftspeople.
  3. Sports in every ward: playing fields rehabilitated, county leagues and talent scouting in athletics and football.
  4. A Nyamira Diaspora Office for investment, skills transfer, medical missions and support to families at home.
  5. Elders' councils involved in peace-building and community dispute resolution.

Pledge: an annual Nyamira Diaspora and Investment Homecoming every December.

Questions

What people ask us

Gideon has worked abroad. Does he understand Nyamira today?

He was born in Manga and grew up in Nyamira's villages, and his family and roots are here. Working abroad gave him experience running systems that work. He comes home regularly, campaigns on the ground, and builds this plan on the county's own budget documents and on what residents tell him.

How will you pay for all this?

By stopping leaks before asking for more. Digital revenue collection, open procurement, paying the right price, finishing stalled projects and maintaining what we build all free money for services. We will also bring in partners, the diaspora and national programmes. Every plan will be costed in the county budget and published.

What will be different about how you govern?

Everything will be measured and published, including drug stock, road kilometres, pending bills and revenue. Projects will be chosen with residents at ward forums. Staff will be hired on merit and held to performance contracts.

Why does healthcare come first?

Health is the largest county function and the service residents complain about most. It is also Gideon's profession. A working hospital is the clearest proof that a county government works.

How can I share my ward's priorities?

Come to a campaign meeting, call the campaign line, or tell a volunteer in your ward. Your priorities will feed into the ward plans.
